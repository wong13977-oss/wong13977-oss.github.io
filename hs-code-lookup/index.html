How do you find the HS code for a product, and its US, EU and UK tariff codes?

Updated 2026-10-14 · By Wong Xian Qi

To find the HS code for a product, describe what it is, what it is made of, how it is made and what it does, match that to a heading and subheading of the Harmonized System, then pick the national line for the country you ship to. The free official sources are the USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule (US HTS), the UK Trade Tariff (UK commodity codes) and the EU's TARIC database. For a whole catalogue, an API such as the AI HS Code Classifier on Apify can do the first pass, which a person should still review.

Where can I look up HS, HTS, TARIC and UK commodity codes for free?

  • United States (HTS, 10 digits): the USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule. The US base duty is its General (column 1, MFN) rate.
  • United Kingdom (commodity code, 10 digits): the UK Trade Tariff on GOV.UK. It covers the UK Global Tariff, with third-country duty and standard UK VAT.
  • European Union (TARIC, 10 digits): the EU's own consultation website is ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/dds2/taric/. The UK Trade Tariff also runs a Northern Ireland service whose tariff applies EU TARIC measures. The tool below uses it for EU results, so confirm EU codes on the official TARIC site before you declare goods.

How do the General Interpretative Rules pick the right heading?

The Harmonized System is applied through six General Interpretative Rules (GRIs), used in order:

  1. GRI 1: the wording of the headings and the Section and Chapter notes decide first.
  2. GRI 2: covers incomplete or unassembled goods and mixtures.
  3. GRI 3: if more than one heading fits, (a) the most specific description wins, (b) for composite goods and retail sets the essential character decides, (c) otherwise the heading that comes last in numerical order applies.
  4. GRI 4: goods the earlier rules cannot place go with the most akin goods.
  5. GRI 5: covers cases and packing.
  6. GRI 6: the same logic one level down: compare only subheadings of the same level.

This is a paraphrase, not the legal text. The deciding facts are the product's essential character, main material by weight, function and how it is made, such as knitted or woven. In the sample below, a vacuum-insulated bottle goes to heading 9617 rather than the household-article heading 7323 because it is the more specific description (GRI 3(a)).

What are the limits of looking codes up by hand?

Manual lookup is fine for a few products, but a catalogue means reading chapters, notes and subheadings for every SKU and repeating the search for each country. The 6-digit code is also only the start: in the sample, a men's cotton T-shirt under 610910 has ten other US lines besides the selected one, and the right line depends on facts such as colour and trim that a title may leave out.

Why does an automated HS code still need human review for customs?

  • It is not binding. Only customs authorities issue binding classifications: US CBP rulings, EU Binding Tariff Information and UK Advance Tariff Rulings. The importer remains responsible for the declared code.
  • It is not always right. The developer tested it on 89 real CBP rulings from 2024 to 2026, which cover hard-to-classify products: the exact 6-digit code matched 70% of the time, the correct code was among the answer and its alternatives 85% of the time, and the exact code matched 90% of the time at confidence 0.85 or higher.
  • Missing facts change the answer. A result is flagged needsReview when confidence is below your threshold or a national line is missing, and a selection of ai-assumed means the AI assumed a fact, which the note names.

Is the duty rate shown the duty I will pay?

No. It is the base rate: the US General (column 1, MFN) rate and the EU and UK third-country duty. It excludes US additional duties under Chapter 99 (such as Section 301, Section 232 or other tariff actions), anti-dumping and countervailing duties, preferential rates under trade agreements, quotas, excise and import VAT. The UK standard VAT rate is shown separately. Check the official tariff or your customs broker before you declare goods.

FAQ

How do I find the HS code for a product?

Describe the product's material, function and how it is made, find the matching heading and subheading in the official tariff using the General Interpretative Rules, then pick the national 10-digit line for the destination. The USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule and the UK Trade Tariff are free official sources.

What is the difference between an HS code, an HTS code and a TARIC code?

An HS code has 6 digits. The US HTS, EU TARIC and UK commodity codes extend it to 10-digit national lines, and each destination has its own duty rates.

Does the duty rate include Section 301 tariffs?

No. The rate is the base rate: the US General (column 1, MFN) rate and the EU and UK third-country duty. It excludes Chapter 99 additional duties such as Section 301 and 232, anti-dumping and countervailing duties, trade-agreement rates, quotas, excise and import VAT, except the UK standard VAT rate it shows.

Is an AI-generated HS code legally binding?

No. Only customs authorities issue binding classifications, such as US CBP rulings, EU Binding Tariff Information and UK Advance Tariff Rulings. The importer remains responsible for the declared code, so review flagged results and check with a customs broker before you declare goods.
